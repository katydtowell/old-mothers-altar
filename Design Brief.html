Design Brief · Section I of IV

Problem Statement

Old Mother's Altar is a private, ritual-minded mobile app for practitioners of witchcraft and folk magic — a spell journal and tracker that takes the work as seriously as the practitioner does.

This brief establishes the design foundation: what the app must accomplish, who it serves, how it compares to existing tools, and how users move through it.

The Problem

Serious practitioners have no purpose-built, private tool for their working life. Generic notes apps lack ritual structure. Existing "witch apps" skew toward reference libraries, wellness aesthetics, or social features. None treat the practitioner's own record as the primary object.

The result: practice is fragmented across handwritten notebooks, phone notes, screenshots, and spreadsheets — with no unified history of what was cast, when, under what conditions, and what came of it. The spell is remembered; the pattern is lost.

What This App Must Accomplish

  • Serve as a private, secure, offline-first spell journal. All data lives on the device. No account required. No cloud sync. Biometric lock available. Notifications use discreet wording. The practitioner's record is theirs alone — including those in the broom closet.
  • Enable creation of spells with full ritual metadata. Each spell should capture intent, ingredients with amounts and notes, timing affinity (moon phase, day, season, time of day), chant or spoken words, deities invoked, duration, and a sealed/draft state — not just a title and text block.
  • Record cast history as a first-class concept. Every time a spell is performed, the event is logged with a date, notes, task completion, and outcome. Multiple casts of the same working build a real history over time. The practitioner can see what they've done and what came of it.
  • Provide a running journal of practice. Beyond spells, the practitioner records notes, observations, and dreams on a shared timeline — all linked to their broader practice, not siloed in a separate app.
  • Integrate live magical calendar data. The current moon phase, current sabbat, Mercury retrograde windows, and eclipse dates surface automatically in the home view and as context when logging entries. The practitioner never has to look this up.
  • Manage ingredient and supply inventory. A cabinet of herbs, crystals, candles, and cords — tracked by type, quantity, and notes — so the practitioner knows what they have before the ritual begins.
  • Deliver an aesthetic that honors the practice. Dark by default. Serifed. Candlelit. Terse. The visual language of a working grimoire, not a wellness dashboard. No gamification, no streaks, no coach-y copy, no exclamation marks.
  • Work fully offline. Moon calculations, journaling, spell management — everything must function without internet access. At the altar, in the woods, wherever the practice calls.

What This App Is Not

  • Not a wellness or mindfulness app
  • Not a social platform, community, or sharing tool
  • Not a pre-written spell library or encyclopedia
  • Not a shop, marketplace, or affiliate surface
  • Not gamified — no streaks, badges, completion rates, or achievement systems
  • Not a beginner's guide — it assumes the practitioner knows their work

The Gap

The serious private-practice journal for this audience does not exist. The market is occupied by reference libraries (pre-written spells, herb databases), wellness-adjacent trackers, and generic notes apps repurposed by practitioners who have no better option. Old Mother's Altar is built for the practitioner who has outgrown all of them.

Cross-Persona Pattern

All three personas share: rejection of gamification, demand for genuine privacy, and the expectation that the app treats their practice as serious — not as a wellness activity or aesthetic hobby. The tone, visual language, and data model of Old Mother's Altar are calibrated to serve all three without condescending to any.

Gen Z's draw to witchcraft is documented as a response to institutional distrust, chronic burnout, and a need for structures they control. The app should never feel like another institution demanding compliance.

Three competitors assessed across Old Mother's Altar's core feature set. Feature data drawn directly from App Store listings and published user reviews.

The closest current competitor. Launched April 2026, built for the same serious-practitioner audience: offline-first, biometric lock, no social features, no pre-written spells. The most feature-complete rival.

Where OMA Wins

The Cabinet — no equivalent exists

No competitor tracks ingredient inventory. For practitioners who work regularly with herbs, crystals, and candles, knowing what they have stocked is a genuine practical need. This feature has no direct rival in the category.

Cast history as a first-class concept

Digital Book of Shadows logs follow-up prompts. OMA logs every cast as a distinct, timestamped event in a timeline — a real practice history. The difference between a reminder system and an actual record.

Depth-first, not breadth-first

Every existing app leads with what it provides (pre-written spells, databases, tarot decks). OMA leads with what the practitioner brings — their own spells, their own casts, their own record. The tool is a vessel, not a library.

Aesthetic: candlelit altar vs. fantasy prop

Midnight-blue-and-gold reads as Hogwarts merchandise. OMA's warm near-black with tarot pigments reads as a working altar at night. Practitioners who take the work seriously will feel the difference in the first second on screen.

Live magical calendar, fully integrated

Sabbat dates, Mercury retrograde windows, eclipse events, and the current moon phase surface automatically in the home view. No competitor integrates all four. This is not a feature — it is the ambient context of the practice, made visible.

UX Flow Outline

Primary navigation structure and key user flows for Old Mother's Altar, based on the current app build — May 2026.

App Structure

Navigation Pattern

A hamburger menu (top-right of every screen) opens a full-height side drawer listing all five sections. There is no persistent bottom tab bar — the drawer is the navigation. Back arrows handle in-section routing. A home icon appears on non-home sections for fast return.

Five Primary Sections
I Home — Today view, ambient dashboard
II Grimoire — Spell library
III Journal — Practice timeline
IV The Cabinet — Supply inventory
V Settings — Profile & preferences
Screen destination screen Action user action or trigger Terminal end state / saved Destructive confirm required

Flow I — Home

The ambient entry point. Greets the practitioner by name with time-of-day awareness, surfaces the current moon phase and live magical events (sabbats, Mercury retrograde, eclipses), shows the last three journal entries, and lists pending tasks from active spells. Two primary actions live at the bottom of every Home view.

Delete Entry → Confirm → Removed
Home → New Journal Entry (bottom) → Journal Entry Editor
Home → New Spell (bottom) → Spell Composer
Home → Tap pending task → Spell Detail (routes to linked spell)

Home — Elements

  • Time-of-day greeting with practitioner's name
  • Live moon phase glyph + label
  • Magical events strip (sabbat · retrograde · eclipse)
  • Recent journal entries (last 3)
  • Pending tasks from active spells
  • New Journal Entry + New Spell bottom CTAs

Flow II — Grimoire

The spell library. Every spell the practitioner has written lives here — searchable by name or tag, filterable by sealed / draft status, sorted by recency. Each card shows name, color, moon phase affinity, tags, cast count, rating (when set), and a favorite heart. Tapping the heart marks a spell as a favorite (solid heart) or removes it. The full spell composer is a separate page, not a modal sheet.

Spell Composer — Fields

  • Name + intent summary
  • Icon + accent color
  • Moon phase affinity
  • Timing tags: season, time of day, day of week
  • Magic type + category tags
  • Rating (1–5 stars, outline when unset)

Flow III — Journal

The practice timeline. All recorded activity surfaces here in reverse-chronological order: cast entries (linked to spells), freeform notes, and moon observations — all on one timeline. Each entry can carry multiple timestamped note blocks, an outcome field, task-completion state, and an image attachment.

Flow IV — The Cabinet

Ingredient and supply inventory. The practitioner tracks what they have — herbs, crystals, candles, cords, and anything else they work with. Type, quantity, and free-text notes per item. No equivalent exists in any competitor app.

Flow V — Settings

Profile and preferences. Minimal and direct. No account management, no subscription, no social profile — just the practitioner's name, their practice note, the deities they work with, and the theme toggle. The app summary (spell count, entry count, cabinet count) is visible here.

Data Architecture Note

All data — spells, journal entries, cabinet items, and profile — is persisted in localStorage on the device. No account. No sync. No cloud. The practitioner owns their own record entirely. This is a deliberate privacy and trust decision, not a technical limitation to revisit.

All data persists across sessions and app restarts. A future export-to-file feature (JSON or vellum PDF) is a natural extension that maintains the same offline-first, no-account philosophy.
